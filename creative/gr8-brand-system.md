# GR8 — Brand System (modeled on ARIH)

Reference brand: ARIH (Korean-inspired, BTS-associated, multi-category CPG, premium look at mass price, Walmart rollout). I like its branding and positioning and want to model GR8 on it. Note: arih.com would not load from the session, so this is read off a shelf photo and market knowledge. Verify the positioning lines against their live site before quoting them anywhere.

## The core lesson
ARIH sells a brand, not a product. It is a strict design system that different flavors and categories wear. The brand is the asset, the SKUs are interchangeable. Model GR8 the same way: a GLP-1 companion platform with a locked design system, not one powder in a bag.

## The six moves

| Element | ARIH does | GR8 move |
|---|---|---|
| Brand model | One brand across many categories | Change: design GR8 as a companion platform. Hero powder now, room for stick packs and hydration later, same wordmark on all. Build the system to extend, not the catalog. |
| Design system | One wordmark, color equals flavor, matte | Keep the figure-8 green mark. Add: lock one layout and make each flavor its own bold color block. Lemonade owns yellow, the seasonal exotic owns its own color. Matte finish. |
| Look versus price | Looks premium, sells cheap in mass retail | Keep the premium look. Design the pack so it could sit on a Sprouts or Whole Foods shelf later, even while it only sells DTC now. |
| Cultural anchor | Korean identity plus a borrowed fandom | Add: GR8 needs a tribe, not K-pop. The "did the shot right, kept everything else" identity. Active, confident, rebuilt-stronger people. That is the fandom equivalent. |
| Function language | Postbiotic, zero sugar, shown as clean specs | Keep structure-function discipline. Creatine, protein, nutrients shown as confident minimal callouts on pack, never as loud health claims. |
| Category naming | Renames the category: Modern Noodle, Postbiotic Energy | Add: coin your own. Not "supplement." Something like "GLP-1 companion" or "the daily for the shot." Naming the category is the big-brand power move. |

## Positioning
ARIH modernizes tired categories with two-word category names. GR8's version is to own a category nobody has named yet: the daily you take with the shot. "Lose the weight. Keep everything else." carries the emotional half. The ARIH lesson adds the structural half: say what kind of thing GR8 is, in coined words, on every surface.

## Design system to lock (so I say it once and reuse)
- Wordmark: GR8, one typeface, same lockup on every SKU and every ad.
- Mark: the glowing figure-8 green symbol, always paired with the wordmark.
- Color equals flavor: each flavor is one bold solid color block. Lemonade = yellow. Seasonal exotic = its own color.
- Finish: matte, premium, lots of negative space, big type, no clutter.
- Function callouts: clean minimal specs on pack (creatine, protein, nutrients), structure-function only, never a loud claim.

## Risks
- Platform vision is right, but do not build five SKUs now. ARIH launched wide on capital and a retail deal. GR8 has one bag. Design the system to extend, ship only the hero, or burn cash and multiply churn.
- The borrowed-fandom trick cannot be copied cheaply. ARIH rented an existing tribe. GR8 has to earn one with the "kept everything else" story, which is slower. Do not fake it.
- ARIH wins on a shelf, GR8 wins on a feed. The minimalist pack still has to carry a painted problem-or-outcome hook in the ad, not just look clean.
- A premium lifestyle look does not relax the GLP-1 ad rules. Same compliance guardrails.
