# iMeow Companion Robot — "Worst Sitter Ever"

**Format:** 15 seconds · third person · no voiceover, no dialogue
**Angle:** firing the cat sitter
**Captions:** optional, added after generation — the ad must read without them

---

## Scene 1 — The sitter takes over (0–5s)

**Kitchen.** Sitter standing at the fridge, door wide open, phone in one hand, pulling food out with the other.

**Cut to living room.** Coffee table buried — chip bags, half a cake, plates, three drinks. Sitter sprawled on the couch, feet up on the table, scrolling.

Behind her, ignored, the cat sits on the floor watching. Push focus to the cat.

---

## Scene 2 — Agitation (5–10s)

Cat pacing the floor in front of the couch, back and forth, tight and fast. Tail whipping. Sitter's feet still up, phone still in her face.

Cat jumps onto the couch back and walks the length of it right behind her head. She leans away without looking up.

Cat drops down, paces again, then springs onto the coffee table — drink goes over the edge. No reaction. Cat is already off it.

Bolts across the room. Launches at the shelf, misses the landing, plant comes down, dirt across the floor. Doesn't stop moving.

Straight into the couch arm — clawing hard and fast, ears back, whole body into it.

**Hold on the wide:** cat tearing up the couch arm on one side of frame, sitter scrolling on the other. She never looks up.

---

## Scene 3 — The tour (10–15s)

Hard cut. No door, no exit shot. Slow tracking shot following the robot on a continuous path through the house. Everything clean, bright, sunlit.

1. **Past the kitchen** — fridge closed. Cat trotting behind the robot, following it.
2. **Past the coffee table** — cleared, nothing on it. Cat now moving ahead of the robot, glancing back at it.
3. **Into the living room** — past the empty couch where the sitter was sprawled. Laser dot drops onto the floor. Cat launches after it, tail up.
4. **Settle** — cat sitting beside the robot, both facing camera.

---

## Production notes

- **Match framing between Scene 1 and Scene 3.** Same camera height, same angles, opposite state. The rhyme is what sells the transformation without a caption explaining it.
- **The final wide in Scene 2 is the strongest frame in the ad.** Cat destroying the couch and sitter scrolling in one composition, no cut between them.
- **The sitter never reacts.** Not once, in any shot. Her total absence of response does more work than a reaction would.
- **No reaction shots from the cat after any spill.** The moment the cat looks at the mess, it reads as intentional revenge instead of a bored animal.
- **Cat behind the robot, then ahead of it.** Reads as the cat choosing to follow. Keep the glance back.
- **If generating:** one continuous 5s tracking shot will likely break. Shoot as three separate location clips with matched camera motion, cut on the robot passing through frame.
- **Callback (optional):** plant upright and intact on the shelf as the robot passes.

---

## Open notes (from review)

- **Clarify the robot is a cat *companion/play* device, not a cleaner.** In Scene 3 the fridge-closed / table-cleared beats risk reading as "the robot tidied up" (Roomba misread). The **laser dot** is the beat that tells the viewer the robot *engages the cat* — lean on it; consider a small play beat earlier so the product's purpose is unmistakable.
- **Add an end card.** Currently ends on the cat + robot facing camera with no product name / price / CTA. For a sales ad, add a closing product card (name · from $X · Shop now). Skip only if this is purely a top-of-funnel awareness piece.
- **Carry Meta compliance forward:** enable AI-generated-content disclosure, no platform UI mimicry, and launch as a new ad in a **separate test ad set** (not one with purchase history).
- **Robot consistency:** keep the robot's design identical across the three Scene-3 location clips (same model shot / reference) or the tracking cut won't feel continuous.
