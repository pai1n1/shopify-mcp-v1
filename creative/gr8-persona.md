# GR8 — Buyer Persona (research-backed)

Tags: [data] = sourced figure (via search summaries, verify before external use). [guess] = inference. Sources were blocked for direct read, so treat exact percentages as directional.

## Age
- [data] Gen X over-indexes most, 35% of users vs 25% of adults. Millennials lead current use, about 18% vs Gen X 17%. Highest use group is women 50 to 64, 20% have used.
- [data] Dense center for a wellness companion subscriber: Gen X and older Millennial women, roughly 38 to 52. Aim the middle, reach the edges.

## Shows and media
- [data] Over-index on TikTok, Reddit and Instagram. Trust influencers and podcasts for health info over doctors. Instagram is where wellness creators live. Podcasts are high trust and high recommendation, so host-read ads convert.
- [guess] Creators that fit: dietitians and doctors doing GLP-1 education, women documenting their journey, menopause and women's-health voices, clean-eating and Trader Joe's haul creators, gut-health accounts.
- [guess] Podcast lane: women's health and hormones, self-improvement and wellness, dietitian-hosted GLP-1 shows. The Mel Robbins, Huberman, mindbodygreen adjacency.
- [guess] TV is ad-supported streaming and lifestyle or documentary, but that is reach, not conversion. Buying happens on Instagram, TikTok, podcasts, Reddit.
- [guess] Winning creative: short, face to camera, dietitian-credible, label-forward, "my daily stack on GLP-1." Not glossy brand film.

## Location
- [data] Prescription hotspots are the rural South and Appalachia (WV, KY, LA), but that is the lower-income base, not the buyer. Buyer over-indexes urban, 41% vs 28%.
- [guess] Target affluent urban and inner-suburban where clean grocery clusters: SoCal, PNW, Austin and Dallas, Denver, Scottsdale, Atlanta, Northern Virginia, Chicago North Shore, NYC metro, South Florida. Target by affluence and clean-grocery density, not the state prescription map.

## Gender and beliefs
- [data] Women majority, 22% ever-used vs 14% of men. [guess] For a clean companion supplement, skew even more female. Lead women, keep a secondary male angle on protein and muscle.
- [data] Pro-self-optimization, 80% build their own routine, read labels, distrust hype, reward specifics (grams, named ingredients, third-party testing). Skeptical optimists.
- Frame they accept: companion, not alternative. They took the shot, they will take the right supplement. Sell protection through the transition, muscle, nutrients, skin and hair.

## The persona
Mara, 44, San Diego, about $120k household. Six months into a GLP-1, paying out of pocket. Reads every label, skeptical of marketing, pro-optimization. Worried about muscle loss, hair and nutrient gaps. Finds products on Instagram Reels, TikTok, dietitian creators, a women's-health podcast, and Reddit. Already subscribes to protein and a wellness product. Pays $69 a month if the label proves itself, a credible expert vouches, and she can cancel easily.

## Male persona (secondary)
- [data] Men are about 14% ever-used vs 22% of women, roughly 35 to 40% of the base, and over-index on protein and performance framing. Underserved, since nearly every competitor is women-coded.

Marcus, 47, Dallas, about $140k. On Zepbound to lose weight, but his real fear is ending up soft and skinny-fat, losing muscle he built. Former lifter, wants to come off the drug stronger not smaller. His door in is creatine and muscle preservation, not wellness. Wants proof and specifics, grams and third-party testing, no patience for fluffy branding. Clean-label to him means no junk and nothing that hurts his labs, not an aesthetic.

- [guess] Where he watches: YouTube, Reddit lifting and fitness communities, X, performance and longevity podcasts, the Huberman and strength-creator lane. Far less Instagram wellness than Mara.
- [guess] Creative for him: face to camera, data-forward, "protect your muscle on GLP-1," strength over skinny.
- Use: Mara is primary. Marcus is the wedge, run a separate creative set on muscle and creatine to the male segment. Do not water Mara's down to fit him.

## Gen Z verdict
- [data] Fastest-growing cohort but off a tiny base, teen and young-adult dispensing up about 600% from 2020 to 2023. Mostly intent and discovery, not sustained paid use, and notably skeptical of these drugs.
- [guess] Not a buyer for a $69 a month subscription now. Low income, low sustained use. An awareness and future layer, not a paid cohort. Do not price or build for them, watch them age in.

## Money note
[data] She already spends $200 to $500 a month on the drug itself, so anchor GR8 as a small add-on to that spend, not a standalone cost.
