# E-commerce Growth OS

Purpose: a living process so each store is better than the last. Claude forgets between sessions, so the memory lives here, in this file, not in the model. Read this first, update it last.

Rule tags: [V] verified by my own data. [T] testing, not proven. [H] hypothesis, unverified.

---

## The loop
1. Read first. Start every session by reading the doctrine and decision rules below, before acting.
2. Apply. Run the store or the campaign by the rules.
3. Capture last. End every session or campaign by writing what I learned back into this file. Add a dated changelog line. Promote a rule from [T] to [V] when the data backs it. Kill a rule the data breaks.
4. Compound. Each new store starts from the latest version of this file, so knowledge stacks instead of resetting.
5. Reconcile, never duplicate. If I say something again, do not add a second copy and do not cancel the first. Match it to what is already here. Same thing, leave it. An update, edit in place and add a changelog line. A conflict, newest wins and the changelog says what changed. I never have to remember what is stored, the file does that for me.

## Voice and language
Governs all copy, angles and market sizing.
- Speak the customer's language. Use the words customers actually say, from their reviews and conversations, never invented jargon or terms they would not believe or use.
- If I do not have their words yet, mark it a gap. Do not fill it with language they do not speak.
- This applies to the TAM too. Size and describe the market in the customer's terms, not a foreign vocabulary.

---

## Customer read (run for every product)
Before building, go where customers already talk and listen. Reviews, forums, comments, groups.
1. Read reviews of similar products, the 1 to 3 star ones most of all, that is where the real wants and complaints sit.
2. Read the forums, comments and groups where this customer gathers.
3. Pull their actual words, what they struggle with, what they want, what they love and what they hate.
4. Feed it in. This sets the voice, the angles and the reads, in the customer's own language, not mine.
5. Where the words are missing or a source is blocked, mark it a gap, do not fill it with invented language. The fix is pasting the raw review text, or reading it in my own browser.

Rule: no product's copy or angles are final until a customer read has fed them. Re-check each cycle, wants shift.

---

## Competitive read (run for every product)
Document the similar brands and feed it into the overall analysis. This is input, not a mandate.
1. Map the field. List the brands selling a similar product, direct and adjacent.
2. Note what they do and what they do not do. Keep these as observations, not verdicts.
3. Feed it in. Treat these as variables for the analysis, ways to help the customer relate, buy, believe, and feel part of the brand.
4. Surface openings softly. Where there is one, note it as something I could push or own, a suggestion to weigh per product, not a commitment I have to make.
5. Serve the goal. Everything here points at converting best, not at planting a flag for its own sake.

Rule: every product in the Store log carries its competitor notes as inputs I can draw on. Re-check each cycle, the field moves.

## Meta ads doctrine
My rules. Each new learning edits this section.

Delivery and traffic quality
- Objective is purchase optimization. The goal is buyers, not clicks or plays. [V]
- Do not recruit bots or junk. Creative decides who clicks, the algorithm only sorts within that pool, so a bad click population cannot be fixed downstream. [V]
- Bid caps. Use them to hold cost per result and to keep delivery off the cheapest, lowest intent inventory. Set, watch, adjust. [T]
- Placement exclusions. Cut the junk placements, Audience Network first, then review under Breakdown, Placement and exclude what does not convert. [T]
- Andromeda 2 is Meta's updated delivery model. Treat its behavior as unknown until my own data shows it. Do not copy tactics from posts, test them on my account. [H]

Creative strategy
- Diversity means different, not more. Better ads for more people, different angles between ads, never a bigger pile of the same ad. [V]
- Cohesive funnel. Every ad has one job that serves the one shared goal, a sale. [V]
- Each ad must do one of three things: answer a question, give a reason to buy, or give information they did not have that strengthens buying intent. [V]
- One ad, one job. If an ad cannot state its job in a line, it is not ready. [V]

New rules go here as I learn them.
